the story

A food delivery platform for Damascus where the captain collects cash at the door. The founder came with a name and a requirements document, no restaurants, no brand and no team: so the scope paper came first, then the product across its four surfaces.

Food delivery Android + Web Cash on delivery Dispatch Scope paper Damascus

about the client

A Damascus founder with a marketing background, launching food delivery in one city, paid in cash at the door. I wrote the scope paper, thirteen sections from the captain's cash wallet to weekly settlement to dispatching the nearest captain, and designed the product across its four surfaces: the customer app, the captain app, the ops panel, the restaurant panel. The offer went out in August 2026.

the challenge

  1. 01

    Cash is the system: the captain pays the restaurant from his own pocket and collects from the customer, so his balance decides who gets the order, and every fee splits 80/20.

  2. 02

    Dispatch without a dispatcher: the nearest free captain with enough cash, an acceptance window, and automatic reassignment when he refuses or runs late.

  3. 03

    No brand to design for: only a name, so the identity had to come with the screens, and read in half a second on a captain's phone in traffic.

the approach

One scope paper the client could sign, then one design system for four surfaces: chili as the only accent, Changa for anything read across a counter, and a bite out of every tile and pill. Android in Kotlin, the web in React, Spring Boot behind them, and a free map stack.

my role

Scope, pricing and the product design across all four surfaces; the offer, its two revisions and the meeting.

movement 02 · at the counter

At the counter

The restaurant page opens on the shawarma counter itself, and the customization sheet asks about the bread and the extras, nothing more. The cart shows one way to pay because there is no other, and derives the delivery fee from the distance in front of the customer instead of announcing it.

The captain pays the restaurant from his own pocket and collects from the customer at the door. So his balance is the first question before any order is assigned.

movement 03 · the ops room

The ops room

A board the shift stares at for eight hours, not a dashboard: three dense columns with the delivered strip folded away, and a drawer that opens on the order with everything a supervisor needs: the address and the note, the captain and his balance, the items, the totals and the commission, and the log minute by minute.

the story

An Android ledger for one shop cashier in Damascus: every invoice, expense and refund typed as it happens, two currencies never converted, the day sealed at close and one Excel file sent to the supervisor.

Android · Kotlin Jetpack Compose SQLite · WAL Two currencies Excel with formulas One user

about the product

Qayd is an Android app for one shop cashier in Damascus: every invoice, expense and refund typed as it happens, in pounds and dollars kept side by side and never converted; at close he counts the drawer, seals the day and sends one Excel file to the supervisor. No server, no internet, a backup that survives an uninstall.

the challenge

  1. 01

    One user with zero tolerance: the worst outcome is a wrong number, so every screen is a ledger first and money is whole pounds and cents, never floats.

  2. 02

    Two currencies with no rate: the app must never add them, total them or convert them.

  3. 03

    A phone that dies mid-entry: the draft has to survive the power cut and the day has to survive a force-kill.

the approach

Kotlin and Compose with an integer-only money model, write-on-save transactions on SQLite in WAL with full sync, a serialized draft queue, daily snapshots into the phone's documents, an append-only audit log, and a dependency-free Excel writer that puts live formulas in the supervisor's file.

my role

Product owner, designer and the only engineer, for one real user: the requirements interview, the design system, the app, the Excel writer, the release.

movement 02 · the day is sealed

The day is sealed

At 14:29 the drawer is counted: 30,170 expected, 30,050 in notes. The difference is 120 pounds short, and the dollars match. Qayd writes the deficit into the day's file and seals the day anyway; a short count is never a locked door.

One user. Zero tolerance for a wrong number.
